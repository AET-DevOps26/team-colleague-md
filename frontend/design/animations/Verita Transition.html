Skill Induction Heads: mechanistic evidence for few-shot learning in 70B models

Key points generated · 4h ago
  • The authors isolate a class of ~140 attention heads across layers 18–32 that fire only on coherent in-context examples — not on shuffled or noisy ones.
  • Ablating a single head drops 5-shot accuracy by 11–18 pts on MMLU-Pro; ablating the cluster collapses few-shot to zero-shot performance.
  • Heads form three sub-populations: retrieval, format, and predict.
  • The circuit appears only above 30B parameters; smaller models show statistically null versions of the same heads.

For the last three years, in-context learning has been the most useful and least understood capability of large language models. You show the model a handful of examples, it learns the pattern, and it does the task. There is no weight update. There is no obvious place where the “learning” could be happening. And yet it works — sometimes startlingly well, sometimes inexplicably not at all.

In this paper we present mechanistic evidence that few-shot learning in 70B-class models is implemented by a specific, localizable circuit: a population of attention heads we call skill induction heads. We characterise the circuit, ablate it, and show that without it, in-context learning collapses to chance-above-baseline.

What we found

Across two open-weights 70B models and one frontier closed model accessed via logit lens, we identify roughly 140 attention heads — concentrated in layers 18–32 — that activate selectively when the model is presented with a coherent in-context task.

The heads are not activated by shuffled, label-corrupted, or off-task demonstrations. They are activated by anything the model can plausibly use as a worked example — even in a language the model has never been finetuned on, even if presented as a comment in a code block.

What this isn't

This is not a claim that we understand in-context learning. We have localized a circuit that is necessary for it; we have not shown that the circuit is sufficient.

The honest version is: we can now point at the place where the magic happens. We still don't know what the magic is.

That said — pointing matters. For the first time, the field has a falsifiable target. The point of this paper is not to be right. The point is to be specific enough to be wrong.
